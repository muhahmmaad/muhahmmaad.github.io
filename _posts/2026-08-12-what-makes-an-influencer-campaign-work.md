Most briefs I receive open with a follower count. It's the easiest number to
find, and it's usually the least predictive thing about whether a campaign will
work.

## Fit beats size

A creator with 15,000 engaged followers in a specific niche will almost always
outperform a 300,000-follower generalist for a targeted release. The smaller
account's audience actually came for that subject. The larger one's audience
came for something else and tolerates the rest.

Before I approach anyone, I check three things:

1. **Comment quality.** Are people responding to the content, or dropping emoji?
2. **Posting consistency.** Sporadic accounts have unpredictable reach.
3. **Audience overlap.** If five creators share the same followers, you're
   paying five times to reach one group.

## Brief for outcomes, not deliverables

"Post one reel and two stories" gets you one reel and two stories. Telling a
creator what the campaign needs to achieve — and letting them decide the format
— consistently produces better work, because they know their audience's habits
better than any brief does.

## Measure what moves

Impressions are a vanity metric on their own. The numbers worth reporting are
saves, shares, profile visits, and whatever the actual conversion event is —
streams, sign-ups, ticket sales. Those survive scrutiny in a way that reach
never does.

None of this is complicated. It's just less convenient than sorting a
spreadsheet by follower count.
