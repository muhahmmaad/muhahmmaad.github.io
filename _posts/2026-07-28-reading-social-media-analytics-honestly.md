When I managed the social accounts at Lahore Business School, I inherited a
monthly report that had been going out for a year. It was full of green arrows.
It also had almost nothing to do with whether the accounts were working.

## Growth without context is noise

"Followers up 8%" means nothing without knowing what happened that month. If a
post went viral for reasons unrelated to the institution, the number tells you
about luck, not strategy. I started pairing every growth figure with what caused
it — a campaign, a share by a larger account, or nothing identifiable.

That last category is the important one. Being honest about *"we don't know why
this moved"* is what makes the months you do know credible.

## Engagement rate needs a denominator you chose

Engagement rate against followers and engagement rate against reach tell
different stories, and it's tempting to quietly report whichever looks better.
Pick one, state it, and stay with it. A metric that changes definition when the
results are bad isn't a metric.

## The question a report should answer

Not "did the numbers go up" but "what should we do differently next month". If a
report can't produce a decision, it's decoration.

I cut that inherited report from six pages to two. The shorter one got read.
